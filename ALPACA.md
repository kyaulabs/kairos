# Alpaca hosted paper

Kairos 0.2.0 adds an optional Alpaca adapter for USD crypto and listed US stocks/ETFs. Orders go to Alpaca's paper service, not Kairos's local simulator. The trading host is fixed to `https://paper-api.alpaca.markets`; live Alpaca execution, funding and account resets are unavailable.

1. Create a dedicated, unused Alpaca paper account and paper API credentials. Do not reuse an account with positions, order history or another order manager.
2. Add `ALPACA_PAPER_API_KEY` and `ALPACA_PAPER_SECRET_KEY` to your private environment configuration using `.env.example` as a reference. Do not overwrite an existing environment file or commit credentials.
3. Set `ALLOW_ALPACA_PAPER_TRADING=true` to permit simulated broker orders and cancellations. Kraken live-trading flags do not enable Alpaca. Paper credentials and account eligibility are still required.
4. Safely restart the service after changing its environment. Back up the data directory first. On a new installation, `KAIROS_EXCHANGE=alpaca` selects Alpaca at startup; otherwise the dashboard's saved exchange takes precedence.
5. Open the KAI sidebar. Stop the current engine, reconcile its orders and close its holdings before changing **Exchange**. Switching never sells positions, transfers balances, resets either portfolio or starts the destination engine.
6. Choose a supported market and strategy. Set the local USD allocation, caps and daily-loss limit, then save. The initial Alpaca profile allocates $500 locally, with $100 order/exposure caps and a $12.50 daily-loss limit. This does not fund the broker account. The allocation can be changed before the first successful account binding, but cannot be silently resized afterward.
7. Press **Start** and confirm hosted paper. Kairos checks the broker account identity, available cash, positions and history. Every service restart remains paused and requires confirmation before resuming.

| Market | Supported execution | Data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| USD crypto | HTF pullback, market making, Bollinger scalping, DCA, TWAP, rebalancing | Free Alpaca US execution-book stream and REST bars |
| Listed US stocks/ETFs | Long-only, whole-share DCA, TWAP and rebalancing | Free IEX quotes/bars; not consolidated NBBO |

Equity orders are regular-session limit orders only. Slice sizes must afford whole shares; TWAP also needs at least one whole share per slice. Schedules use elapsed wall time. Closed-session and missed slots are skipped rather than queued or caught up. Mixed equity/crypto rebalance baskets wait for the equity session. Equity HTF/scalping, fractional shares, shorts, margin, options and arbitrage are not supported.

Crypto passive entries use a freshly checked, non-crossing GTC limit. Alpaca does not provide the Kraken post-only guarantee used by Kairos. There is no taker fallback after a passive miss. Cancellation is local: **GTC orders can remain working if Kairos stops unexpectedly or loses connectivity.** On restart, automatic checks look up outstanding orders without replaying or canceling writes. Unresolved orders block Start; use Stop to request cancellation of tracked working orders. Stop retains holdings; local protection does not operate while stopped or disconnected.

Crypto planning uses 25 bps on both sides, the published entry-tier taker rate, rather than promising the maker discount. Equity planning uses zero commission; Alpaca's paper documentation excludes regulatory fees. These are labeled estimates, not authenticated fee-tier reads. The order API does not report per-order fees. Kairos books posted `FEE`/`CFEE` account activities exactly once and adjusts crypto ownership for base-asset fees. Portfolio totals include those posted fees; order and run fee attribution is unavailable. Scheduled budgets include conservative planning fee reserves. Unexplained balances stop execution instead of being guessed. BTC/USD spot HTF has a bounded [pending-fee accounting path](TRIAL.md): verified debits remain separate from actual fees, and running entries reserve possible unposted costs. Other strategies retain strict settled-balance checks.

Do not assume paper crypto trading is fee-free or that every debit will receive an activity. [Alpaca's crypto documentation](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/crypto-trading#crypto-spot-trading-fees) describes fees in the credited asset and end-of-day activity posting. Its [paper documentation](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading) excludes regulatory fees, not all crypto fees. On 2023-08-31, [Alpaca Developer Relations reported paper fees being applied without appearing in activities](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/portfolio-value-fluctuating-with-no-open-positions/12819/4). That older discussion concerns regulatory fees; it does not establish today's crypto posting behavior. A [2025 paper-ETH user report](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/paper-trading-ethereum-fees/16890) also describes reduced credited quantity, but is not an official guarantee. These sources were checked on 2026-10-05. Kairos therefore keeps verified debits unclassified and reserves possible remaining costs. Absent journals alone do not block normal qualification or explicit trial Start; account, ownership and risk checks still apply. Records older than 48 hours warrant investigation, without inventing a fee or releasing its reserve.

Only the selected exchange runs. Kraken keeps `kairos.sqlite3`; Alpaca uses `alpaca-paper.sqlite3` under the same `DATA_DIR` lock. Settings, ledgers, protection, schedules, daily baselines and order history remain separate. Back up both databases and preserve the directory lock. Browser favorites are also separate. Alpaca order history cannot be deleted or archived through Kairos because reconciliation depends on it.

An unused paper account can report its initial balance as a `JNLC` cash journal, often described as `Journal cash between accounts`. At first binding, Kairos accepts a single executed USD cash journal only when its positive amount equals the account cash and there are no orders or holdings. The full activity becomes part of the baseline; later corrections still halt reconciliation. Existing initial `CSD` deposit handling is unchanged.

If the initial journal appears after binding, it remains blocked until explicitly reviewed. The Accounts activity view includes its date, status and description. With trading paused, an operator can POST `/api/reconcile` with the exact `initial_funding_id` and `confirmation: "ACKNOWLEDGE INITIAL PAPER FUNDING"`, using the normal Origin/JSON/CSRF checks. This repair requires an empty saved activity baseline, unchanged initial allocation and account cash, no local or broker orders/holdings, and two matching broker read passes containing only that journal. It records the full activity and an audit event without changing money, settings, risk baselines, protection or trading permissions. Back up the store first. It is not a general deposit, withdrawal or account-reset acknowledgement.

Reconciliation rejects external orders/fills, unexplained cash or positions, account identity changes, corrected activities and unsupported corporate actions such as splits or dividends. These need operator investigation; automatic verification cannot adopt unrelated holdings or discard records. Routine checks remain automatic. The separate unused-account initial-funding acknowledgement and Kraken reconciliation are unchanged.

For a stopped, interrupted BTC qualification with a known local holding, **Reconcile manual sale** previews an external liquidation and requires confirmation to record it. Two matching complete broker reads must prove one extra, fully filled sell of the entire owned quantity, a flat account, matching fill proceeds and bounded cash differences. Other orders, transfers, partial sales and changed evidence remain blocked. The action records the existing order and gross fill atomically with its acknowledgement and the archived failure. It cannot submit/cancel orders, reset a baseline or resume trading. The sale is labeled external, not a Kairos submission. Its reconciliation fee bound comes from the qualification's captured allowance, not a historical fee quote. Only actual activities classify fees.

Ambiguous submissions are looked up by their durable client ID and are never blindly resubmitted. A missing lookup remains unresolved. Activity reconciliation scans from account binding and is bounded at 10,000 records; reaching that bound halts rather than silently truncating history. This initial adapter is not intended for high-volume order workloads.

Alpaca crypto HTF uses `alpaca-us-native-bars-v1`: 30 adjacent, completed provider-native bars at the configured candle interval. With the saved 60-minute setting, these are UTC-aligned hourly bars from Alpaca US, not rolling windows reconstructed from 1,800 minute bars. Native hourly completeness does not assert that every underlying minute was published. Real zero-volume bars are valid; missing, malformed, unordered, duplicate or forming native bars cannot authorize entries. A delayed latest bar is retried on the existing review cadence, never replaced with the previous hour. The old minute archive is retained unchanged; Kraken's rolling-minute policy is unchanged.

The default `pullback-v1` policy remains available. The separately registered [`multibar-v2` paper trial](TRIAL.md) separates a completed-bar setup's lifetime from short-lived execution permission. It requires the authorized execution qualification before the user's first Start; deployment never activates it.

New setup transitions require a newly observed completed native bar. Intrabar quote changes cannot create a new setup; legacy pending setups still face fresh quote, spread, cost, confidence and risk checks. Start and data recovery re-baseline entries rather than buying an already-active signal. Bounded model reviews retain their short expiry, not an hour-long approval. Independent target, stop, loss and deadline checks still run each engine cycle and do not require historical bars or model approval; they still need fresh execution quotes. Run records identify the history policy, and observation events retain the native input bars, fetch time and content hash. This data-policy change requires a new run and is not profitability evidence.

Chart bars exclude the forming interval. Browser quotes are a bounded subset of the catalog, refreshed when a market is selected. The stock picker shows up to 100 search matches. 24-hour price changes remain unavailable. Volume refreshes at most every five minutes, only for browser favorites and the watched chart market. Crypto volume is USD turnover, summed as volume × VWAP for each completed five-minute bar over 24 hours; it is Alpaca US data, not global volume. Stocks/ETFs show IEX-only daily USD turnover (shares × daily VWAP), with the session date in the tooltip. A missing VWAP does not fall back to the latest price. Favorites in the footer show prices only; their volume cache remains available to the chooser. The chart-market chooser only displays volume cached through those watch subscriptions; browsing, searching and sorting do not subscribe other markets. Removed favorites retain their cached values without refreshing. Values older than ten minutes are marked cached; missing data stays unavailable. This cache is shared across browser tabs and cleared on service restart. Account views are read-only; order/activity views show at most the latest 100 records, not a complete export.

Hosted paper does not establish execution quality or profitability. Alpaca's simulation does not faithfully model queue position, market impact or available liquidity. Verification covers API-shaped fixtures, risk/reconciliation failures and browser behavior, plus unauthenticated public crypto data reads. Fixture tests do not establish authenticated account eligibility or broker-hosted fill behavior. Verify those separately during an explicitly started paper trial.

## Market-data recovery

Crypto execution uses one shared connection to `wss://stream.data.alpaca.markets/v1beta3/crypto/us`, subscribed only to the configured execution markets. Browser searches, chart selections and favorites do not expand this execution subscription. The stream applies full resets, price-level updates and zero-size deletions; disconnects invalidate old stream books. Reconnects back off from one to 60 seconds. Authentication/subscription failures and connection-limit errors stop reconnect attempts until explicit feed restart or market reconfiguration. Credentials are sent only after checking the final handshake URL. No Kraken-location substitution or paid feed is used.

REST remains the bootstrap/fallback, limited to one execution-book attempt per market per five seconds. Equities continue to use IEX REST top-of-book. A recent response, heartbeat or unchanged price does not refresh the source timestamp. Both source age and time since local receipt must satisfy the saved depth-age limit, including another check immediately before the order POST after any quota wait. Passive quotes are also rechecked for crossing. This does not provide Alpaca post-only protection or guarantee a fill.

While explicitly started, a stale book, disconnected stream, market-data transport failure, HTTP 429 or selected market-data 5xx response can enter a **data wait** lasting at most five minutes. No stale-data orders are allowed. Account reconciliation continues, and local exits may be blocked while quotes are unavailable. Fresh data clears only this read-side wait, then strategy/risk checks run again. HTF entries take a new baseline instead of chasing an outage-era setup; existing ownership, stops and deadlines remain. Explicit Stop, loss halts, uncertain orders and malformed inputs cannot auto-resume. With scheduled recovery disabled, exhaustion also requires operator recovery. The opt-in flat-account retry stage described below does not clear any of these hard halts. Scheduled slots already claimed before a read failure remain claimed, without catch-up or duplicate orders.

All REST callers share a priority admission queue: orders/cancellations/lookups first, then account reconciliation, execution data and finally dashboard reads. It admits at most 150 requests per rolling minute, with ordinary reads capped at 130 and dashboard reads at 30, while retaining 350 ms spacing. The remaining 20 admissions are reserved for order-critical work. Published/observed server limits and `X-RateLimit-Remaining`, `X-RateLimit-Reset` and `Retry-After` can further restrict admission. Data and trading backoff are tracked separately; another application's usage can still exhaust the account quota. Queue waits are bounded, expiring intents are rejected before transmission, and writes are never automatically retried. Ambiguous writes still require durable-ID reconciliation.

**Review** reports each execution book's source, market timestamp/age and local receipt time, plus stream errors, HTTP endpoint/status/latency, queued requests, quota observations/reset/backoff and the bounded data-wait state. Unknown quota values stay unavailable. Admission counts include preflight-rejected attempts and are conservative budget usage, not broker-accepted order counts.

Account-read failures have a separate recovery path. While explicitly running and armed, timeouts, connection failures, quota deferrals, HTTP 429 and selected 5xx responses from the account, positions, order-list, activities and clock GET endpoints can trigger up to three full read-only reconciliation attempts. Attempts start at least 15 seconds after the previous failure, on the engine cadence, with a 120-second retry deadline. There are no retries inside an individual HTTP request. Start and dashboard reads do not start this running recovery schedule. Separate paused account checks run at least 60 seconds apart, with 300-second spacing after failures. They cannot submit/cancel orders or resume trading.

New orders and local protective exits are blocked until the full account pass and current loss check succeed. HTF entry approvals are then discarded and entries take a new baseline; ownership and protective plans remain. Active or uncertain orders, an existing recovery/loss latch, malformed data, TLS verification failures, permanent HTTP errors and storage failures cannot auto-resume. Stop takes precedence. With scheduled recovery disabled, exhaustion leaves trading paused. Automatic checks must verify the account before an explicit Start/Restart. No POST or DELETE is retried by this recovery path; order-specific lookups remain outside it. Existing quote freshness, risk and short model-approval limits still apply on the next normal cycle.

The optional `api_auto_recovery` setting adds five read-only recovery attempts after the short account/data budget expires. The first starts no earlier than five minutes later; each failed attempt waits another five minutes. Each full pass is bounded to 60 seconds and checks account identity, positions, orders, activities, loss limits, any equity clock and fresh execution books. All checks must pass together. It is available only to an already running, armed account last reconciled flat, with no active/uncertain orders or recovery/loss latch. Orders remain blocked. Success re-baselines HTF entries and resumes ordinary checks on the next cycle, never an old order intent. Stop cancels the schedule. Service restarts do not restore it. Permanent failures or five failed attempts halt for investigation and an explicit restart after successful verification; no write is retried.

The dashboard distinguishes running (including running with pending fees), waiting for account/data, scheduled retry, halted and paused states. Review shows automatic account-check results and period-specific observed debits, posted fees and reserve caps. Risk equity/P&L with additional pending-fee allowances is labeled provisional; it is not a posted-fee total. Review includes transition time, service-session durations, last evaluation, retry count/next attempt and Discord delivery status. Durable operating-state events and minute health samples include session IDs; service downtime is not running time. While paused or waiting, a bounded read-only recorder can retain completed native bars and source diagnostics. It does not reconcile accounts, call Jev, create candidates or grant execution permission. These are diagnostic observations, not missed-trade claims.

Set `KAIROS_DISCORD_WEBHOOK_URL` privately in the service environment to enable halt, scheduled-retry and restoration notifications. Use a permission-0600 environment file or your existing private configuration; never put the URL in chat, a commit, command history or a dashboard setting. Only `https://discord.com/api/webhooks/<id>/<token>` is accepted. Delivery uses a bounded queue, a five-second timeout, no redirects or automatic retries, and disabled mentions. Messages contain a generic state and direct the operator to the authenticated dashboard, not account data or raw errors. The dashboard reports disabled, invalid or failed delivery; notification failure never permits a trade.

Review shows the last full successful account reconciliation, failed execution-read endpoint/reason/time, recovery attempts, duration and outcome. These snapshot fields cover the current service session; `account-read` history events retain failed passes and episode outcomes without headers, payloads or account identifiers. A successful unrelated market-data request does not clear the recorded account-read failure. A transport failure on a GET means the read failed, not that an order was submitted.

Alpaca documents event-driven quote/book streaming, not a guaranteed sub-10-second update schedule. A read-only BTC/USD probe on 2026-10-01 returned 24 successful REST responses, but only four of 12 samples per endpoint met a 10-second market-age limit. Streaming reduces delivery delay and REST load; it cannot force a quiet or delayed source to update. Keep freshness gates rather than treating download time as market time.

Fee-period evidence uses the [account activity reference](https://docs.alpaca.markets/reference/getaccountactivities-2). Alpaca distinguishes a non-trade activity's `date` from `created_at`; API date filters select creation dates, which for fees are typically the following UTC day. Kairos scans complete bound-account activity history rather than polling only an assumed posting date. UTC audit snapshots do not prove that Alpaca has finished posting fees.

Sources: [crypto stream](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/real-time-crypto-pricing-data), [stream protocol and connection limits](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/streaming-market-data), [latest order books and quota headers](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/cryptolatestorderbooks-1), [Trading API usage limit](https://alpaca.markets/support/usage-limit-api-calls). The published Trading API limit is 200 requests/minute/account; the public crypto probe also advertised 200/minute. Runtime response headers take precedence over those observations.

## Review and first operational trial

Open the hamburger menu and select the **Review** icon on the vertical rail. The panel shows only the selected section; use the rail icons or Arrow Up/Down, Home/End to switch. It uses saved settings and existing engine snapshots, not chart selections or unsaved form drafts. Opening it makes no broker requests, adds no market subscriptions and cannot save settings or start orders. Review remains available while trading; Stop stays reachable above the tabs.

The report separates repeated entry checks, cost-qualified checks, order records and fills. A partial fill is also a filled order; these counts are not additive. Missing historical inputs remain unavailable. Alpaca HTF readiness reports completed native bars and the native-bar shortfall, not synthetic minute coverage. Kraken's HTF minute shortfall measures the additional consecutive suffix needed, not the number of holes in the entire archive. Paused/stale history is not entry authorization. Program quantities are separated by market and side; archived records still count. Unspent DCA allowance includes planning reserves and future slices, not just freely available cash. Posted Alpaca fees remain account-wide, without invented order/run attribution.

For a first operational trial with a dedicated $10,000 paper account, use **BTC/USD DCA** and a **$500 local allocation**. This tests scheduling, bounded orders, fills, fees and reconciliation. It is not a profitability recommendation or evidence that DCA beats holding cash or BTC.

| Setting | Trial value |
| --- | --- |
| Exchange / execution / product | Alpaca / hosted paper / spot |
| Bot market | `alpaca:BTC/USD` |
| Strategy | DCA · scheduled accumulation |
| Paper starting balance | $500 local allocation, not $10,000 |
| Slice notional including planning fees | $10 |
| Slices / period | 7 / 86,400 seconds |
| Engine cycle | 60 seconds |
| Order cap / exposure cap | $10 / $100 |
| Daily loss limit | $12.50 |
| Reinvest / capital recovery | Both off |
| Slippage / maximum spread / depth age | Keep defaults: 10 bps / 30 bps / 10 seconds |

Save these settings before Start. If a previous program exists with different terms, stop and reconcile it, then explicitly create a **New strategy run**; never reset balances or erase history. Start validates and binds the unused account. Assuming $10,000 available cash, $9,500 remains outside the fixed $500 allocation. No broker funds are transferred. The seven planned slices total $70 including planning reserves, not $500 or $10,000. The first slice is due immediately and the seventh six days later if the engine remains online. Prices, market minima, risk checks and available liquidity may prevent or reduce fills; do not increase limits merely to force activity.

DCA uses bounded IOC limits, not guaranteed fills or passive GTC entries. Review requested versus filled quantities, retained assets, working orders and reconciliation errors after the first slice and daily afterward. Missed slices are not caught up automatically. Completion does not sell BTC, and DCA creates no automatic stop/target exit plan. Stop and the daily-loss halt do not flatten inventory or guarantee a maximum loss. Leave a failed run stopped for investigation; do not automatically restart or rearm. The earlier reporting-only release did not submit authenticated trial orders. The separate qualification in [TRIAL.md](TRIAL.md) requires explicit authorization and does not establish strategy profitability.
